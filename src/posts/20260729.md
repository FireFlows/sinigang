It's the end of July, meaning school's back in session next month. It'll be my first year of college... kind of. 

I've always been bad at school. I had bad grades, the teachers had issues with me, and none of the kids wanted to even look at me; I wondered in elementary school if I was invisible, since no one paid attention to me. I would try to get my peers' attention, but they ignored me every single time.

I was also one of those kids who received the designation of "gifted" in elementary school. I took an IQ test, and while they didn't tell me my IQ, they did tell me I was "gifted in reading but not in math." 

So every week, on a certain day, at a certain time (lunchtime), my school would hold a sort of gifted club for those who were identified as gifted. In North Carolina, gifted kids are part of the "AIG" — **A**cademically **I**ntelligent and/or **G**ifted program. However, I was usually not allowed to attend these clubs, as I was a bad bad child who did not do their homework and was defiant, so I can only describe them vaguely. ~~It doesn't help that I'm way past elementary school years.~~

In middle school, I was handed a paper from Duke University telling me that I could take the SAT early, since I only missed one question on my reading/English/ELA end of year standardized test. However, I was not able to take this because the pandemic started right around then. (Six years ago? Time seriously flies, like.)

I continued to be in the AIG up until high school, where I think they stop doing that shit and just move on to AP and Honors classes. I liked AP Statistics, the only AP class I ever took. I took a many honors classes, but did bad at them. I was bad at every subject, including English. My worst subject was chemistry, and I don't remember a single thing from it. 

I don't like chemistry. Whenever I asked chemistry questions, I was made to look like a dumb idiot — and I kind of was. Saying "I don't remember a single thing from it" is a bit of a lie; it's more like there's nothing *to* remember. I never processed a single word. I was at the back of the class playing games.

Well, I still graduated, so it's probably fine. ~~With a 2.3.~~

My high school took the ACT, not the SAT. I did incredibly well in the English/Reading section, but average in the Math/Science one (Writing wasn't included on my test).

If you asked me before about my favorite school subject, I'd say "art" or "English," but now I usually say "nothing, because I hate all school subjects." It's cringe and stupid but I genuinely cannot give a real answer. It's true. I hate it. School is, was, and continues to be, a torture chamber for autistic and ADHD children, before they know they have either.

When I was around seven years old, I took an ADHD evaluation by my teachers' recommendation. However, the doctors came to the conclusion I did not have it, so I continued my nightmare for eleven more years. At 18 years old, in January 2026, I was diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder and attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder.

Perhaps my ADHD didn't come through to the doctors at the time; kids love running and jumping around, etc. I have inattentive type ADHD (ADHD-PI), which isn't as visible as hyperactive. Maybe it's because I'm Asian? It's hard to say. I can't go back in time twelve years ago to ask them why I wasn't diagnosed with ADHD. 

By the time I was diagnosed, my first semester of college had already passed. Can you guess how well I did?

I either failed or withdrew from all my classes. My GPA was at a resounding 0. Zero point zero. 

College is strange. In high school, I was able to get up at like 7 AM ~~most of the time~~, but now I can barely drag myself out of bed at 12 PM. Classes are short, and you're not forced to be on campus for 7 hours a day, so most assignments are technically homework. I'm not bad at homework, necessarily, just bad at *doing* homework. This is a common pitfall for many ADHDers. They're very motivated and passionate... about the wrong things.

In the middle of February, I was practically forced to withdraw from my second semester classes since I was not gonna make it. I thought I could handle it but I couldn't. But despite that, I was so happy. I was freed from college. I didn't have to go to school. I didn't have to get a job. I didn't have to do anything except sit on my ass all day. 

My birthday is near the end of the school year. I love celebrating my birthday, but I don't like thinking about the end of the school year, since it means coming face-to-face with my bad grades. Luckily, this year, I was able to just focus on the positives.

But all good things come to an end, and now I have to go back to college. In my about page, I say I want to become a translator for Japanese. I want to major in linguistics, minor in Japanese, and study abroad in Japan. I can't do any of that if I don't go to college, so to fulfill my dreams, I have to do it.

But, also, I love learning. Linguistics has been an intense interest of mine for a while, and it would be great to get an actual diploma in it. Since I'm a hobbyist, I might relearn a lot of things, but I hope to be taught many things too. Plus, I get to prepend all my posts with "as a person with a degree in linguistics..."

Maybe learning about something I'm actually interested in will give me a leg-up.

I'm nervous and excited for the semester. I have accommodations set in place, but the medicine I was given doesn't work. I feel nothing when I take it. So, truthfully, I haven't been taking it.

My GPA is still a 0. But it's not as bad as it looks: I withdrew from most of my classes, and only failed one or two. Apparently, if I start doing well enough, even my school records can pretend like my first semester and second half-semester never happened.

Still, I'm very close to being expelled. I'm on academic probation right now, since my GPA is less than 2.0. So, I'm going to try not to get expelled this semester.
